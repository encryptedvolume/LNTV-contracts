## Scope and threat model

Reviewed `src/RankedAuction.sol`, `src/AuctionEdition.sol`, `src/RoyaltyMarketplace.sol`, the inherited ERC-721 transfer/approval/receiver paths, `script/Deploy.s.sol`, deployment inspection, the audit gates, mutation runner, invariant models and local deployment rehearsal. The pinned source hashes in `results.json` identify the production revision. All three production files are unchanged from the previous reviewed revision.

An attacker may submit many bids from many wallets, control NFT/ETH receiver contracts, order its transactions adversarially, reject callbacks, make callbacks across contracts, force ETH into the contracts, and invoke public settlement/refund-credit functions. The payout wallet may change through its authorized nomination/acceptance flow; it has no authority over another bidder's refund or winning claim, nor another seller's credit. Supply, pricing, deployment bindings, the royalty rate and metadata base are fixed. The metadata operator controls off-chain content, so content secrecy, rarity and reveal permission are outside this contract security boundary.

The business rules reviewed are 90 ranked auction NFTs plus ten reserved NFTs; top rank pays its full bid; other winners pay the lowest winning bid at capacity or reserve below capacity; original bid ID breaks ties; the initial duration is 24 hours; new bids at capacity require a ceiling-rounded 5% floor increment; existing bids use a ceiling-rounded 0.5% increase; late new bids and rank-changing increases leave five minutes with no cap.

## Manual review

| Area | Verification and conclusion |
|---|---|
| Ranked list | Insertion and unlinking preserve head/tail and reciprocal links, active count, descending amounts and original-ID tie priority. Only an active bidder can increase its bid; a displaced bid cannot reenter through an increase. Operations are bounded by the 90-place book. |
| Bid arithmetic | Amount narrowing and increases reject values above uint128. Floor and increase calculations operate in uint256; multiplying a uint128 amount by 500 cannot overflow. A computed minimum above the bid limit disables new bids without disabling settlement. |
| Auction timing | Creation/increases require start <= timestamp < current end. Settlement allows timestamp >= current end. The extension deadline uses uint256. Raising an unchanged rank intentionally does not extend. Time advancement is now exercised inside the randomized models, as well as boundary unit tests. |
| Tiered settlement | For N > 0 winners, proceeds equal highest bid + (N - 1) × regular price. Every winning bid covers its assigned cost, so proceeds cannot exceed winning escrow. N = 0 avoids subtraction underflow; N = 1 charges only the full highest bid. Final rank fixes token IDs before any mint receiver is called. |
| ETH accounting | Before settlement, escrow is the sum of active bids. Displacement moves value into the original bidder's refund balance. Settlement moves only sale revenue into proceeds. Refund crediting moves only overpayment from escrow to refunds; duplicate entries cannot credit twice. Withdrawals debit liabilities before interaction and roll back on failure. |
| Mint allocation | Winning IDs 1..N, unsold IDs N+1..90 and reserved IDs 91..100 are disjoint. Allocation counters are consumed before safe minting and revert with failed callbacks. Only the immutable auction can mint. A failed recipient cannot block other claims or refund withdrawals. |
| NFT claims | Only the original winning bidder can claim and redirect its wins. Duplicate or mixed-owner claim batches revert atomically. Refunds remain the original bidder's entitlement after minting and resale. |
| Secondary sales | Listings bind seller, token ID, price, expiry and transfer nonce. Actual ERC-721 owner/approval checks execute on transfer. Every secondary transfer, including self-transfer, advances the nonce. Exact ETH payment is required and a failed transfer reverts credits, royalties and listing consumption. |
| Transfer enforcement | ERC-721 public transfer paths reach the overridden update hook; unauthorized transfers and attempts to mint through transferFrom revert. New approvals are limited to the immutable marketplace. Royalties round up and cannot exceed consideration at the accepted rate. |
| Wallet rotation | Nomination does not transfer authority; only the pending wallet may accept. Acceptance clears the nomination and changes business-revenue/inventory control, without moving bidder or seller entitlements. All 14 recorded Slither warnings were reviewed again against the same source. |
| Cross-contract callbacks | A mint receiver can legitimately sell a received NFT through the marketplace. Rejecting a subsequent mint in the same batch must roll back that sale and its payment. A buyer's receiver can accept an authorized payout nomination, but cannot withdraw royalties while a marketplace sale is executing. New tests exercise both success and rollback paths. |
| Deployment | The script validates chain, lead time, numeric bounds, nonzero wallet, metadata suffix and collection labels. Construction binds all three contracts atomically. The checker compares runtime and repeated immutable slots, then validates bindings/configuration and solvency at one block with a stable block hash. |

## Verification gaps addressed

These are improvements to test assurance, not newly discovered exploits:

1. **Callbacks across contract boundaries:** added four focused tests covering a resale during safe mint, rollback of that resale if a later NFT is rejected, payout acceptance during a purchase, and rollback of the payout acceptance if the purchase is rejected.
2. **Post-settlement ordering:** added a fuzz test that shuffles winning claims and interleaves refund crediting/withdrawals after proceeds have already been withdrawn, including full and partial subscription. It verifies every final NFT ID and drains all ETH and all 100 mint entitlements.
3. **Time in stateful campaigns:** added auction-clock advancement with an independent expected deadline and independent minimum-bid calculation. The market campaign now advances time and accounts for expiry when selecting purchases. Campaigns still include no-op cases; handler-call counts are not counts of successful transactions.

The test additions were first checked in an isolated copy before inclusion in the final suite. The audit runner now accepts `AUDIT_FUZZ_SEED` and prints the seed used, preserving reproducibility when expanding exploration beyond the previous seed.

## Documentation findings

Two descriptions needed clarification and were corrected without changing the contracts:

- **Custody/wrapper control — Informational:** the README's blanket claim that wrapping is prevented was too broad. `claimTokens` lets the bidder choose a recipient and `AuctionEdition.mint` accepts compatible ERC-721 receiver contracts. Such a recipient can transfer its own control or issue transferable economic interests while `ownerOf` of the underlying NFT remains unchanged; the NFT transfer hook cannot charge that separate transaction. The README and primary report now explicitly limit enforcement to underlying NFT transfers through the included marketplace. Restricting recipients to EOAs would break contract wallets and still would not prevent private-key sales, so no recipient restriction was introduced.
- **Revocation versus cancellation — Informational:** approval revocation temporarily prevents a listing from filling. Restoring approval can revive an otherwise valid listing, as the existing `testRevokedApprovalRollsBackFillAndCredit` already demonstrates. The operating instructions now direct sellers to `cancel(listingId)` for permanent cancellation.

## Continuing limitations

- No public-chain deployment, live wallet-connected bidding page or independent external audit of this revision is claimed.
- Metadata/reveal hosting, owner authentication and creator enablement are separate work. The on-chain contract cannot enforce those off-chain permissions or prevent the host from changing content.
- All secondary transfers of the underlying NFT require the included marketplace. Direct gifts and external marketplace transfers are blocked; royalties apply to declared ETH consideration and cannot detect side payments, private-key sales or a transfer of control/economic interests in a contract that holds the NFT.
- Active bids cannot be cancelled, and qualifying late bids can extend the auction indefinitely. Demand reduction, shill bids, transaction ordering and multiple-wallet participation remain possible.
- Revoking marketplace approval blocks fills while approval is absent; restoring approval can make an otherwise valid old listing fillable again. Use explicit listing cancellation for permanent cancellation, including before changing revealed metadata.
- The deployment has no upgrade/pause/rescue role. A lost payout key without an accepted or usable pending replacement has no separate recovery path. Winning claims have no expiry, and forced surplus remains inert.
- Automated coverage and mutation checks are finite evidence. They do not establish that all possible defects have been excluded.
